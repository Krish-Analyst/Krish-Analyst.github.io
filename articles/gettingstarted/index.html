Getting Started: What to Do When You Have an Idea But Don't Know Where to Begin

Everyone has ideas. Most people never do anything with them. Here's how to go from idea to actual business without overcomplicating it.

May 2026 · Updated October 2026 · 3 min read · By Krish Panda

Having an idea is the easy part. Knowing what to do first is where most people get stuck. These are the steps that matter most at the very beginning.

Validate Before You Build

Before spending any money or time building, ask yourself one honest question: would anyone actually pay for this? Not would they say it's cool. Would they pay for it.

Tell ten people about your idea and ask if they'd buy it. Not if they like it. If they'd buy it. The answer will tell you more than months of planning.

Even better, ask for real commitment:

  • Pre-sell it. Show a mock-up or sample and take orders before you produce anything.
  • Name a price. "Would you buy this for $35?" gets a far more honest answer than "Do you like it?"
  • Watch what people do, not what they say. Messages, orders and sign-ups count. Compliments don't.

Start Smaller Than You Think

Whatever you're planning, do a smaller version first. Want to sell clothing? Sell one product before building a full collection. Want to offer a service? Get one paying client before building a website.

The first version isn't supposed to be perfect. It's supposed to teach you what your customers actually want.

Work Out What It Will Cost

Most first products can launch for much less than people expect, especially with print-on-demand, a free store plan and photos taken on your phone. But "probably not much" isn't a plan.

Write down every cost before you spend anything: setup, first stock, monthly tools and a buffer for surprises. The free Startup Cost Estimator adds it all up for you, and shows how many sales it takes to earn the money back.

Price It Properly From Day One

Underpricing is the easiest way to work hard and make nothing. Know your true cost per item, then price for a healthy margin, not just "a bit more than it costs". The pricing guide explains how, and the Pricing Calculator does the maths.

Pick One Thing and Do It

The biggest mistake new entrepreneurs make is trying to do everything at once. One product. One platform. One marketing channel. Get that right before adding anything else.

Set a Deadline

Ideas without deadlines stay ideas. Give yourself a specific date by which you will have something live, even if it's rough. A deadline does more than motivation ever will.

A simple 30-day plan looks like this:

  1. Week 1: validate. Talk to ten potential customers and name a price.
  2. Week 2: make a sample or a first version, and work out your costs.
  3. Week 3: set up a simple store or order form, and take good photos.
  4. Week 4: launch to the people who said yes, and ask them to share it.

Get Your First Ten Customers

Your first customers won't come from ads. They'll come from people who already know you, and from the people they tell. Start with the ten people you spoke to in week one, then friends, family, classmates and online communities where your customers already spend time.

Treat every early customer like gold. Ask what they liked, what they'd change, and whether you can share their feedback. Those first reviews make it much easier to win customer number eleven.

The Only Thing That Matters at the Start

Revenue. Not followers, not a perfect logo, not a beautifully designed website. Your first goal is to get one person to hand you money for something. Everything else comes after that.

"Done is better than perfect. Shipped is better than planned."
